# Cassette 01 — connection research and proposed development details

24 September 2026. **Design-development record; no fabrication or structural release.**

## Decision

Continue with sawn-timber boundary members and supported CNC plywood skins. Develop mechanically fastened seams, bearing joints and separately designed uplift restraints. Do not convert the cassette into a plywood friction-fit kit or copy WikiHouse connectors. The selection favours accessible assembly, inspectable load transfer and replaceable commercial fasteners. Its actual dimensions remain unvalidated.

Studio owns the familiar WikiHouse-style configuration controls. System supplies module geometry, placement rules, interface records and detailed inspection. The default Studio comparison stays at 2,100 mm wall height; the 2,700 mm System study is not an engineered alternative. WikiHouse and Studio v1 remain separate.

## Questions and method

This targeted technical review asks: how do neighbouring cassettes transfer shear, uplift and bearing forces; how can DIY builders reach fasteners; which documented products fit the framing; and what tests distinguish a plausible detail from a validated system?

Compare alternatives by material applicability, experimental support, installation access, reversibility, moisture sensitivity and rights clarity. Prioritize measured behaviour over marketing. Review two experimental papers, selected handbook/design-book sections and three manufacturer documents. This is not a systematic literature review or an exhaustive connector-market survey. Publisher access failed for additional leads already recorded in RESEARCH.md; their snippets are not evidence. No supplier was contacted.

**Labels:** “evidence” describes the reviewed source; “proposal” is OBTP's untested design choice; “hold” identifies missing evidence or engineering. Neither this report nor the displayed contact graph supplies a fastening schedule.

## Evidence register

### C1 — timber connection mechanics

Rammer, D.R. (2021), *Fastenings*, Wood Handbook, FPL–GTR–282, Chapter 8. [USDA full chapter](https://research.fs.usda.gov/download/treesearch/62253.pdf). Selected nail, screw, bolt and moisture-related sections reviewed, not every equation. The technical synthesis links performance to grain, wood properties, penetration and moisture history. It supports mechanical fastening as a principle; US design procedures do not establish Lithuanian design resistance. Link and paraphrase only; embedded third-party rights were not exhaustively checked.

### C2 — panel-scale versus building-scale experiments

Miedziałowski et al. (2023), *Stiffness of Experimentally Tested Horizontally Loaded Walls and Timber-Framed Modular Building*, Materials 16, 6229. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/ma16186229); [full-text XML actually reviewed](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10532801/fullTextXML). Full textual methods, results and discussion now reviewed, extending the earlier abstract-level review. Wall groups used different mechanical fasteners **and adhesive**; the four-storey building combined OSB/gypsum sheathing, engineered members and intermodule connections. Instrumented wall failures and building response provide useful experimental evidence. The whole-building test stopped at finish damage, not structural collapse. Its stiffness exceeded a sum based on isolated walls, but there was no controlled isolation of connector effects; material, restraint and assembly differences confound attribution. Do not transfer its screw spacing, stiffness or capacity to OBTP. Our inference: test adjoining cassettes and a corner/diaphragm assembly, not just isolated screws. Article CC BY 4.0; no figures or proprietary system geometry copied.

### C3 — plywood interlock experiments

Aranha, Hudert & Fink (2021), *Interlocking birch plywood structures*, International Journal of Space Structures 36(3), 155–163. [Full text](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/09560599211022219). Methods, specimen descriptions, modelling limitations and assembly discussion reviewed. Selected short-term tests on exhibition-derived birch-plywood joints do not validate a cassette seam. Pull-out was not investigated, rotational response was limited, and tight fits complicated assembly; linear-elastic modelling does not validate failure behaviour. This argues against assuming an untested friction fit supplies all required restraint. It does not disprove traditional joinery generally. Article CC BY 4.0; no joint drawings copied.

### C4 — European design framework and narrow walls

Swedish Wood (2022), *Design of timber structures*, Volume 2, third edition. [Book PDF](https://www.swedishwood.com/siteassets/5-publikationer/pdfer/sw-design-of-timber-structures-vol2-2022.pdf). Selected sections 9.2, 10 and 11 plus preface/copyright reviewed. This educational interpretation is not the normative standard. Its simplified wall method A requires end tie-downs, uniform perimeter fastener spacing and sheet width at least h/4. It excludes panels containing openings from that method's racking contribution. Connection slip and combined loading require consideration. National parameters are not supplied for Lithuania. Copyright retained by publisher; linked, not reproduced.

**OBTP geometric screening, not resistance calculation:** h/4 is 525 mm at 2,100 mm and 675 mm at 2,700 mm. Nominal 600/582 mm panels pass only that width screen at the lower height. At the taller height neither does, and the horizontal sheet joint introduces another assessment issue. Do not assume tying two cassettes together automatically creates one wide qualifying panel. A Lithuanian engineer must check the actual current standards, national annexes and complete method conditions.

### C5 — real timber-to-timber screw candidate

Rothoblaas HBS. [Product](https://www.rothoblaas.com/products/fastening/screws/screws-carpentry/hbs); [Eurocode technical sheet](https://www.rothoblaas.com/attachments/288905-product-241/hbs-en-technical-data-sheet.pdf). Application, distance and design-principle sections reviewed; complete ETA not reviewed. The product covers timber/wood-panel applications and protected service classes 1/2. Its distances and resistances depend on material, loading and installation conditions. This is a shortlist for paired timber seams, **not selection of a diameter, length or spacing**. Do not use CLT-specific provisions for sawn studs or generic screws in an assessed connector pattern. Manufacturer copyright; buy/use an approved product after design, do not clone its geometry.

### C6 — real angle-bracket candidate

Simpson Strong-Tie ABR. [Official technical/product page](https://www.strongtie.co.uk/en-UK/products/reinforced-angle-bracket-abr). Application, tabulated fastening conditions and installation notes reviewed; complete ETA not retrieved. Tables refer to specified CNA nails/CSA connector screws and, for relevant cases, paired brackets. Halving a paired value for a single bracket requires the stated rotation restraint; eccentricity cannot be ignored. Shortlisted for a designed shear/restraint role at bearing interfaces. Fit, support and load directions remain unchecked. Manufacturer documentation is linked, not copied; no commercial connector model reproduced.

### C7 — hold-down candidate and a dimensional mismatch

Rothoblaas WHT, current new version. [Product](https://www.rothoblaas.com/products/fastening/brackets-and-plates/angle-brackets-and-plates-for-buildings/wht); [technical sheet, marked 07-26](https://www.rothoblaas.com/attachments/298406-product-1226/07_WHT_EN.pdf). Application, geometry, installation and narrow-pattern conditions reviewed, not a complete ETA calculation. The sheet cites ETA-23/0813 and describes a narrow pattern for 60 mm studs. OBTP has 45 mm members: that documented arrangement is **not established as compatible**. Its timber/steel/anchor checks and intermediate-layer conditions cannot be replaced by a bolt drawn through the floor. A larger terminal member or another assessed product requires engineering; adjacent studs are not automatically one solid post. The sheet marks the design registered. No copying/manufacturing licence or freedom-to-operate clearance inferred; no geometry copied.

## Conceptual assembly and envelope sequence

The following is an OBTP planning proposal for prototype review, not site instructions:

1. Design supports, anchorage, lifting/manual handling and temporary stability before erecting any cassette. Module mass and safe handling have not been established.
2. Fabricate open frames with inspectable supported plywood edges. Use CNC for repeatable panel cutting and identification; retain accessible screw-driving faces. Do not machine structural holes until a reviewed schedule exists.
3. Trial adjacent floor cassettes and a wall corner, leaving access to seams and anchors. Record squareness, stock moisture, driver access, repair/reassembly and measured fit. Establish tolerances from stock and trials, not invented nominal clearances.
4. Add roof cassettes only under a reviewed erection/restraint plan. Inspect fastening and bearing before closure; photograph and identify each connection.
5. Complete insulation and continuous air/weather control only after inspection. Separate structural restraint from seals: compressed mineral wool is not an airtight joint or a structural spacer.

For a heated Lithuanian studio, carry forward the **proposed**, unvalidated envelope study from RESEARCH.md: mineral wool within framing, a continuous interior air/vapour-control strategy with a service zone, consideration of exterior continuous insulation, and drained/ventilated weather protection. At seams and corners reserve reachable membrane laps and tape substrates; at plates and anchors detail penetrations rather than assuming a continuous line in a section. Double studs, rims and metalwork need thermal-bridge assessment. Roof falls, water drainage and ground moisture require their own details. Intermittently heated/unconditioned use needs different boundary conditions, not a claim that moisture risk disappears. No membrane type, insulation thickness, vapour resistance, U-value or ventilation rate is released here. The earlier climate sources R5/R6 remain abstract-level evidence, not a verified recipe.

## Alternatives retained

| Option | Why not selected for this first detail-development pass |
|---|---|
| Bolted, accessible panel seams | Plausible for demountability; requires tool access, washers/bearing, slip and timber-movement assessment. Screwed open-frame seams are simpler to prototype, but not proven superior. |
| Continuous top/bottom rails spanning multiple cassettes | Potentially clearer diaphragm/anchorage continuity; adds field pieces and changes assembly. Retain as a strong option if cassette-by-cassette seams cannot deliver the required stiffness. |
| Traditional housed/lapped registration | Could locate parts or supply bearing; section loss and tension restraint still need design. Do not translate a solid-timber precedent directly into a thin-plywood slot. |
| All-plywood interlock | Attractive CNC workflow; C3 does not establish the required uplift, racking or moisture-conditioned performance. Keep separate from the current system. |
| Bonded stressed-skin cassette | Needs controlled adhesive application and quality assurance; no composite action assumed in this DIY prototype. |

## Validation programme before a construction release

**Engineering brief:** fix use/site, supports, timber grade, plywood declaration, snow/wind and imposed loads, moisture/service class, fire and durability. Check 4,572 mm floor/roof spans, vibration, bearing, stability, diaphragms and all load combinations. The displayed 45/195/220 mm dimensions are nominal concept choices.

**Connection coupons:** an engineer/test laboratory should define specimen counts, conditioning and acceptance criteria for the chosen materials/products. Measure lateral slip, withdrawal/head pull-through, splitting and failure modes as applicable. Include dry and relevant moisture-cycled samples; distinguish installation repeatability from strength. Do not adopt numerical pass loads without calculation.

**Subassemblies:** test at least an adjoining-wall seam and a floor–wall–roof corner with the intended hold-down/support arrangement; include reversible lateral loading, uplift and serviceability measurements as required by the engineer. Separately assess sheathing seams, narrow panels and diaphragm edge continuity. Isolate changed variables before attributing improvements to a connector.

**Envelope prototype:** verify accessible membrane continuity, penetrations, water shedding and airtightness; undertake transient hygrothermal analysis for actual climate/occupancy and thermal-bridge calculation. Test ventilation/condensation assumptions before calling the studio habitable.

**Software:** preserve geometry-only tests, explicit null capacities/fasteners, unsupported-opening holds and independent WikiHouse/v1 regressions. A green software check is not a passed physical test. Current CAD shows contact geometry, not screw/bracket installation or a complete load path.
