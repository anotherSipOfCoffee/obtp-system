# Cassette 01 connection alternatives — 24 September 2026

## Decision and status

Continue with mechanically connected timber cassettes. Prefer accessible, inspectable interfaces and direct compression bearing; resolve lateral restraint and uplift separately. These are **research-informed OBTP proposals**, not replicas of tested specimens. The seven local 3D details establish a reviewable connection concept, not a fastening schedule. Amber volumes show fastener zones; violet parts show generic metal connector envelopes. They are not a purchased connector or approved hole pattern.

No paper reviewed validates the complete OBTP cassette. Evidence is used to choose mechanisms, failure checks and experiments. OBTP timber grades, plywood declarations, actions and connector products are still unselected. No resistance or stiffness is assigned. The member dimensions remain the existing nominal design study; local cutaway lengths, 3 mm plate envelopes and fastening-zone extents are original display assumptions, **not values derived from papers**. Their suitability has not been established. A manufacturer product may require a different envelope and different timber sections.

## Evidence-to-decision register

### P01

Islam, M. S., Chui, Y. H. & Altaf, M. S. (2022). Design and Experimental Analysis of Connections for a Panelized Wood Frame Roof System. Buildings 12(6), 847. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/buildings12060847).

Reviewed: Publisher-indexed abstract and material/connection-method excerpts; full article retrieval blocked.

Finding: 37 specimens across five configurations were tested under monotonic loading. Connection orientation and member/fastener pairing form part of the test definition. Application: Use distinct roof, seam and frame-junction test cases. Limitation: Engineered LSL roof details differ from OBTP sawn timber. No tested dimensions, capacities or screw schedules transferred.

### P02

Yan, Z., Ottenhaus, L.-M., Leardini, P. & Jockwer, R. (2022). Performance of reversible timber connections in Australian light timber framed panelised construction. Journal of Building Engineering 61, 105244. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jobe.2022.105244).

Reviewed: University abstract and publisher-indexed methods/failure-mode excerpts.

Finding: Tests of proprietary connectors in MGP10 framing exposed moisture-related fit problems and some connector failures. Application: Check material compatibility, failure hierarchy and installation clearance. Limitation: Australian timber and proprietary hardware are not OBTP details; no connector geometry copied.

### P03

Ottenhaus, L.-M., Yan, Z., Brandner, R., Leardini, P. & Fink, G. (2023). Design for adaptability, disassembly and reuse – A review of reversible timber connection systems. Construction and Building Materials 400, 132823. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.conbuildmat.2023.132823).

Reviewed: Full text available; sections 2.2–2.4, 3.2–3.3 and conclusions inspected.

Finding: Access, timber damage and fastener reuse affect reversibility; repeated screw insertion cannot be assumed to retain performance. Application: Keep inspection access and distinguish removal from verified reuse. Limitation: Review-level principles do not certify this assembly. Article CC BY-NC; link and original summary only, no figures copied.

### P04

Kuai, L., Ormarsson, S. & Vessby, J. (2024). Numerical and experimental investigations of prefabricated light-frame timber modules. Engineering Structures 303, 117528. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.engstruct.2024.117528).

Reviewed: Publisher abstract and university-indexed first page; full PDF retrieval timed out.

Finding: A parameterised FE model was compared with full-size tests at global and local scales. Application: Record joint slip/stiffness and validate assemblies, not just connector counts. Limitation: No FE model executed or calibrated for OBTP.

### P05

Maharjan, R., Kuai, L., Vessby, J. & Ormarsson, S. (2024). An experimental analysis of full scale light-frame timber modules. Engineering Structures 304, 117617. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.engstruct.2024.117617).

Reviewed: Publisher-indexed abstract, introduction, experimental setup and conclusion excerpts; full PDF unavailable.

Finding: Three module designs were examined under combined vertical and lateral loading; connection conditions affected sliding, uplift and racking response. Application: Separate bearing, shear and uplift paths, then test the coupled assembly. Limitation: Different sheathing, framing and boundary conditions prevent importing resistance values.

### P06

Cowled, C. J. L., Slattery, T. P., Crews, K. & Brooke, H. (2023). Influence of Weathering on the Structural Performance of Sheathing-to-Timber Connections. Forests 14(4), 734. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/f14040734).

Reviewed: Publisher-indexed abstract and study-description excerpts; full publisher retrieval blocked.

Finding: The programme compared weathered and unweathered sheathing connections using monotonic and cyclic protocols. Application: Include conditioning and load-slip behaviour in the test brief. Limitation: Different sheathing and nailed specimens; no numeric weathering factor adopted for OBTP screws.

## Connection catalogue

### A01 — Plywood to frame

Supported panel edge with mechanical fastening into framing. Evidence: P06, P04.

Intended paths:

- Panel in-plane shear
- Sheathing fasteners
- Studs, plates and blocking

Assembly access: Fit and inspect from the panel face before closing the weather layers. Leave the taller-wall horizontal seam backed.

Failure checks: Panel tear-out / head pull-through; Timber splitting / fastener yield; Cyclic slip and moisture damage.

Prototype: Sheathing-to-frame coupon followed by full wall racking with the intended panel product.

Adaptation limit: Wall sheathing is 12 mm and floor/roof deck is 18 mm in the existing model. Neither thickness establishes fastening capacity.

### A02 — Wall to wall

Accessible face splice across paired boundary studs; compare with direct screws. Evidence: P02, P03, P05.

Intended paths:

- Sheathing into boundary stud A
- Splice and its fastener groups
- Boundary stud B and adjacent panel

Assembly access: Install at the inside stud faces while the cavity is accessible. Keep a removable service cover; inspect both fastener groups.

Failure checks: Fastener withdrawal / lateral yield; Plate yielding or rupture; Stud splitting and differential slip.

Prototype: Two-panel seam: in-plane and out-of-plane loading; repeat assembly after conditioning.

Adaptation limit: Flat splice is an original candidate, not the proprietary connector tested in P02. A splice does not by itself establish the wall diaphragm.

### A03 — Corner return

Accessible interior angle ties perpendicular end and side wall framing. Evidence: P02, P03, P05.

Intended paths:

- Return-wall end stud
- Angle and separate fastener groups
- Side-wall boundary member

Assembly access: Install from the open interior corner before insulation and lining. Maintain tool access to both legs and space for the airtight corner seal.

Failure checks: Angle opening / prying; Fastener withdrawal and splitting; Corner slip / torsion.

Prototype: L-shaped wall specimen: both lateral directions plus uplift compatibility.

Adaptation limit: Original angle envelope; sheet corner wrap, fire lining and thermal bridge remain separate design tasks.

### A04 — Wall to floor

Bearing carries compression; a separate tie links stud to floor rim/blocking for uplift and shear. Evidence: P05, P02, P03.

Intended paths:

- Compression: stud → bottom plate → deck → rim/blocking
- Tension: stud → tie → rim/blocking → designed foundation anchor
- Shear: wall boundary → designed restraint → floor diaphragm

Assembly access: Locate at a boundary stud with real timber behind both tie ends. Fit before exterior insulation/weather layers; retain an inspection strategy.

Failure checks: Plate/deck compression; Tie or fastener failure; Rim splitting and missing support anchorage.

Prototype: Stud–plate–deck–rim junction under compression, shear and uplift, with coupled actions as required.

Adaptation limit: Existing midpoint interface markers are NOT tie locations. Foundation anchorage is absent; this local tie does not close the building load path.

### A05 — Roof to wall

Direct bearing plus a tie bridging the roof boundary member and wall stud. Evidence: P01, P05, P03.

Intended paths:

- Gravity: roof joist → top plate → stud
- Uplift: roof member → tie → wall stud → base hold-down
- Diaphragm shear: roof edge → wall restraint

Assembly access: Install at aligned members before closing the roof edge. Maintain access without puncturing the final water-control layer unreviewed.

Failure checks: Bearing / cross-grain compression; Tie withdrawal or tear-out; Roof edge splitting and uplift discontinuity.

Prototype: Roof–wall coupon in shear and uplift; then corner assembly with roof diaphragm.

Adaptation limit: P01 provides a connection-test precedent, not a flat-roof detail. Roof drainage and uplift design remain open.

### A06 — Floor / roof seam

Paired edge joists linked laterally; deck edges fastened to their own supporting joists. Evidence: P01, P04, P05.

Intended paths:

- Deck edge → sheathing fastening → edge joist A
- Joist-to-joist connector
- Edge joist B → adjacent deck

Assembly access: Install lateral screws/bolts from an open joist bay before underside closure. Check collision with blocking and access along the entire seam.

Failure checks: Lateral fastener yield / embedment; Splitting and differential deflection; Diaphragm slip and unsupported deck edge.

Prototype: Seam strip in shear plus full diaphragm and differential vertical displacement tests.

Adaptation limit: Illustrated axes are fastening zones, not screw count or spacing. P01 LSL members differ from these 45 mm sawn joists.

### A07 — Frame corner / blocking

Bearing contact plus a mechanical bracket at internal stud/plate or blocking joints. Evidence: P01, P04.

Intended paths:

- Stud or blocking member
- Bearing and bracket fasteners
- Plate or boundary joist

Assembly access: Assemble while frame faces are open; inspect before skin installation. Check bracket does not conflict with sheathing fastening.

Failure checks: End-grain connection weakness; Splitting / bracket prying; Loss of restraint during handling.

Prototype: Representative frame corner and blocking tests followed by handling/erection review.

Adaptation limit: Newly explicit internal joint requirement. Existing box contacts never established fastening of the individual cassette.

## Prototype and measurement brief

1. Record supplier declarations, actual dimensions, timber density/grade, grain direction, plywood lay-up, moisture, installation method and torque limits.
2. Make sheathing coupons and each connection type with the exact proposed production materials. Engineer/laboratory to choose specimen numbers, standards, conditioning and statistical treatment before testing.
3. Measure force versus local slip, residual slip and failure mode in relevant directions. Include monotonic and reversed/cyclic cases where applicable. Test realistic moisture conditions and controlled assembly/disassembly cycles; do not reuse damaged holes as if new.
4. Test a corner containing a floor seam, wall seam, base tie and roof tie. Follow with wall racking and full diaphragm tests. Record both interface movement and whole-frame displacement.
5. Establish a foundation/support connection and temporary bracing plan. Neither is generated in this revision. Correlate any calculation/FE model with measured responses before selecting a release range.

## Scope of the digital checks

Local detail tests check finite dimensions, no positive-volume overlap between timber/plywood/plate envelopes, and intersection of each fastener-zone volume with timber. Zone intersections with their intended members are deliberate. These checks do not evaluate drilled holes, screw penetration, allowable edge distances, steel thickness, tool access or structural resistance. Assembly tests cover all 1–8 module counts and both 2,100/2,700 mm wall heights. The connection graph is a topology check, not structural analysis.
