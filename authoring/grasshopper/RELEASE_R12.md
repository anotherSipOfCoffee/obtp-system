# R12 - coordinated platform, roof layers, component study, supplier schedules
Owner authorised website publication on 2026-09-26. R11 work is retained; Architecture and Drive unchanged.

## Foundation
The photographed R11 base shared axes but was still made of independent rows. R12 uses one `foundation-platform` assembly and transverse ties between actual overlapping bearing rows. Upper floor and drained terrace remain separate materials/levels; a common foundation does not require continuous indoor joists across the external wall. This avoids claiming an unstudied moisture/thermal junction is resolved. Geometric contacts are explicit; anchors, reinforcement, soil, joint stiffness and capacities remain unverified. Concrete dimensions remain provisional envelopes, NOT a Zyle foundation specification.
Longitudinal timber members now run through intermediate supports, max6000mm per stock member, with splices at support axes. No intermediate pile removed. New cross ties can increase total piece count; this is not concealed as a net physical reduction.

Actual plywood end joints now have blocking between rafters. No invented fastener capacity/count. The membrane option remains on continuous18mm plywood; adhesive/mechanical fixing not selected.
Gable total height remains4480mm by recalculating rise from its full assembly. Flat1:40 (~1.43deg) remains below mono8deg. No projected element is called a cut.

## Component study
Retain hierarchy module > cassette > part. Fractal recursion is not a cost function; more subdivisions can add parts and joints. Use dimensional coordination, repeated geometric types, supported long stock, then cutting-stock planning. No structural member removal justified by visual repetition alone.
ISO2848 describes dimensional coordination of components/assemblies; it does not certify a600mm grid structurally or promise fewer parts.
https://www.iso.org/standard/7846.html
Geometric rectangular timber types are normalised under orientation, keeping family/material/sloped geometry distinct. Profiles/grain/grade/holes remain a fabrication review. First-fit-decreasing cutting-stock study uses explicit6000mm stock and3mm saw kerf assumptions, excludes sloped/oversize members; not globally optimal, no2D sheet nesting. Component PDF shows physical pieces, original/normalised types, stock utilisation, quantities and partial cost scope. New roof supports may increase count despite fewer types.
