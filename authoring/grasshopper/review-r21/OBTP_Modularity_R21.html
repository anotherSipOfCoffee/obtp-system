Opening findings and supplier fit

  • 1180 means frame outside width in the actual code. The current window frame is 1180 × 1880 mm, in a 1200 × 1900 mm rough opening with modelled 10 mm perimeter gaps. It is a Pihla coordination candidate, not a verified size/order specification. The public sauna-window page confirms a product family but does not establish this exact glazing/profile/installation combination.
  • Harvia D91902M: manufacturer publishes 890 × 1890 × 92 mm. The model reserves 900 × 1900 mm. This checks envelope arithmetic only; the 5 mm side and 10 mm top allowance is not confirmed as Harvia's installation requirement. Leaf, clear passage and product-specific handling remain unverified.
  • Swedoor 9×19: frame 888 × 1880 mm, approximate installation opening 910 × 1900 mm. Existing generic 900 mm apertures are 10 mm too narrow by that guidance. No substitution adopted.
  • Swedoor 10×21: frame 988 × 2080 mm, approximate opening 1010 × 2100 mm. Existing apertures are short by 110 mm width and 200 mm height. With the retained 2100 mm wall, this also consumes the header zone. A 1200 mm host does not resolve vertical fit or bearing. No entrance narrowed to obtain a nominal fit.

The new canonical scene.modularity.openings records retain rough openings, modelled/verified frame and leaf fields, usable passage, structural jamb zones, header bearing, supported side infill, installation gaps, tolerance status, threshold/sill/floor/head levels and component IDs. Unknown supplier tolerances, finished-floor build-up and clear passage are null, never fabricated values. No unsupported thin closure has been added.

Five distinct layers in the canonical model

  1. Planning: 300 mm coordination reference; retained 900 × 1200 planning cells and existing face datum.
  2. Assemblies: existing structural wall, floor and roof recipes; connected display walls remain separate from manufactured assemblies.
  3. Manufactured components: existing shared identity method, retaining unresolved machining, material grade, grain, handing and connection roles.
  4. Purchased products: explicit units plus reconciled representation pieces and product evidence.
  5. Connections/adapters/terminals: counted existing components and interface records, with unresolved capacity, fasteners and tolerances.

Cutting, handling and remaining decisions

Default metrics JSON includes the existing 6000 mm stock / 3 mm kerf first-fit timber study. This is hypothetical stock, includes kerf in utilisation, and excludes shapes unsuitable for that study. No supplier stock order or 2D sheet-nesting efficiency is claimed. Since selected geometry is identical, material use and cutting results are identical. The PDF assembly shows connected walls flat, at 45°, and upright near their erection line. It does not certify lifting the entire wall; temporary supports, bracing, masses, swept motion and equipment need engineering.

The next meaningful physical redesign requires resolving actual entrance/head-height requirements, exact window products, corner fastening/load paths and factory cutting constraints. Adding smaller cells or deleting apparent duplicate studs before those decisions is not supported by this screen.

Files and execution

Run CREATE_GRASSHOPPER.py in Rhino 8 Python 3 with Grasshopper open to create the R21 native definition. All imported OBTP modules are bundled. Existing controls and the 0–100 assembly/core-frame previews are retained. This cloud environment has portable rhino3dm, but no native Rhino or Grasshopper runtime; a newly generated native .gh and native solve have not been verified here. Included three PDFs are for default Studio M only. Websites were neither built nor deployed.
